About the Author

"Rendering timeless Sanskrit stotras, philosophical treatises, and devotional epics into mellifluous, metered Tamil poetry for spiritual seekers and devotees worldwide."

Sri R. Gururaj (திரு. ரா. குருராஜ்), a Group A Officer in the Operating Branch of Indian Railways, has a profound lifelong devotion to Tamil and Sanskrit spiritual literature. Although a Mathematics graduate by formal training, his deep love for Tamil was nurtured from childhood by his mother, Late Smt. S. Rajam, who was a graduate in Tamil. He acquired foundational knowledge of Sanskrit through courses offered by Samskrita Bharati.

His literary journey began with the publication of “Arusuvai Ramayanam”, a creative work rendering the Ramayana in Tamil poetry across six distinct classical forms (including Samkshepa Ramayanam, Andhadhi Ramayanam, and more). A copy of this book was presented to Maestro Ilaiyaraaja on 09.04.2025. The book was subsequently expanded and republished under the title “Sapta Ramayanam”, incorporating a seventh poetic rendition.

Sri R. Gururaj presenting Arusuvai Ramayanam to Maestro Ilaiyaraaja (09.04.2025)

The Poetic Adaptation Endeavour

With a devotional commitment to share sacred texts with seekers and devotees, Sri R. Gururaj has undertaken the monumental endeavour of adapting 18 classical Sanskrit stotras, philosophical treatises, and epics into metered, rhyming Tamil poetry.

His corpus includes Valmiki’s Samkshepa Ramayanam, the expansive 300+ verse Andhadhi Ramayanam, Sri Venkatesha Suprabhatam, Sri Vishnu Sahasranamam, and Goswami Tulsidas’s Hanuman Chalisa (Maruthi Naarpathu).

Though a follower of the Madhwa tradition (Dvaita), his universal reverence for spiritual wisdom led him to translate several of Adi Shankaracharya’s profound Advaitic masterpieces - such as Bhaja Govindam, Lakshmi Narasimha Karavalambam, and Maneesha Panchakam - inspired by the insightful audio discourses of his Sanskrit Guru, Dr. Uma Maheshwari.

Presentation & Portal Features

Each work is presented with strict fidelity to the original Sanskrit texts alongside poetic Tamil adaptations formatted for daily parayanam (recitation), contemplation, and study, enriched by expressive hand-drawn illustrations.

Sri Vishnu Sahasranamam Special Analysis

A distinctive highlight of this portal is the comprehensive classification and cross-referencing of the thousand sacred names of Lord Vishnu - categorizing repeating names (occurring twice or thrice), opposite names, twin names, names associated with the Dashavatara, and the Keshavadi Namas. Every entry is directly linked to its corresponding shloka and nama, complete with individual word meanings and Tamil poetic commentary.
